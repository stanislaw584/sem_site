# Everyday studies

This section helps you understand how studies work at SEM: where to find the schedule, what the curriculum is, how grades work, and where to find your instructor.

## Curriculum

The curriculum shows which courses you study, when they take place, and how the final grade for each is determined.

The curriculum includes:

- required courses;
- elective courses;
- internships;
- term papers or projects;
- forms of assessment: pass/fail credit, graded pass/fail, exam;
- the number of credit units.

If you do not understand your curriculum, contact the Deputy Head of your degree program or the SEM Administrative Office.

## Courses

A course is an academic subject. Each course has a syllabus, requirements, and a grading system.

At the start of the course, the instructor usually explains:

- which topics will be covered;
- which assignments must be completed;
- how the rating is calculated;
- what deadlines apply;
- whether there will be a pass/fail credit or an exam.

## Pass/fail or exam

The form of assessment shows how the final result for the course will be determined.

- **Pass/fail credit**: the result is either "passed" or "not passed."
- **Graded pass/fail**: the result is a grade, for example "excellent," "good," "satisfactory."
- **Exam**: a separate final assessment, if provided by the grading plan.

In many SEM courses, the final result depends on the rating accumulated during the semester.

## Academic calendar

The academic calendar shows how study periods are distributed throughout the year:

- classroom instruction;
- internship;
- credit week;
- exam period;
- vacations;
- final state assessment for graduates.

The calendar is posted on the FEFU website under **Studies / Academic Process / Class Schedule**.

## Class schedule

The schedule can be viewed:

- in the "My Univer" personal account;
- on the FEFU website under **Studies / Class Schedule**.

The schedule shows:

- course;
- instructor;
- classroom;
- date and time of the class;
- group or subgroup;
- class format.

### What is an "okno" (gap)

An "okno" is a free period between classes. You can use this time for lunch, the library, preparing for classes, or handling things on campus.

## How to reach an instructor

The best first step is to write to the instructor on corporate email.

You can also:

- check the schedule of individual consultations on the FEFU website;
- ask in the department or chair;
- contact the Deputy Head of the degree program;
- if you do not know where to go, contact the SEM Administrative Office.

## Individual consultations

Instructors may have office hours. During this time you can ask questions about the course, assignments, grades, or exam preparation.

The consultation schedule is posted on the FEFU website under **Studies / Instructor Consultation Schedule**.

## Continuous assessment and rating

The rating is the cumulative score for a course. It is built from various assignments and assessment activities.

During the semester, the following may count:

- tests;
- homework;
- practical work;
- projects;
- presentations;
- attendance, if specified in the grading plan;
- mid-term tests.

The current rating can be viewed in the personal account in the **Rating** service.

## The priority is studies

SEM's main recommendation: do not postpone academic problems.

The student is responsible for:

- attending classes;
- completing assignments on time;
- monitoring corporate email;
- checking the rating and grades;
- knowing the dates of the exam period and retakes;
- asking for help before a problem becomes critical.

## Teamwork

Studies often include group assignments and projects. For teamwork, it is important to:

- assign roles in advance;
- agree on deadlines;
- record decisions in writing;
- respect the contributions of other participants;
- not leave the team without a response before a deadline.
